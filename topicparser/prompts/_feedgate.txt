You are given posts that came from an official source: a lab's or a company's OWN
blog, or its own YouTube channel. They already passed scoring. Your ONLY job is to
drop the ones that are the company talking about ITSELF rather than a thing that
happened.

Being first-party is not news by itself. These channels publish releases and
marketing side by side, in the same voice, with the same badge.

ALWAYS KEEP, no weighing of how interesting it is (scoring already decided that):

- the post announces a THING THAT NOW EXISTS from the team that made it: a model, a
  tool, a dataset, an API, a spec, an open standard, a version, a public beta. A
  launch, an update or an access expansion all count. "Introducing X", "Announcing
  X", "Meet X", "X is now available" is ALWAYS this, whatever channel it is on: a
  VIDEO that announces a named product or feature is a launch, never a how-to.
- the post reports a RESEARCH RESULT: a named system, model or method that
  demonstrates, achieves or measures something. The lab's own research announcement
  is the event, even when the write-up reads like a study.
- the post opens something the reader can personally JOIN: an open program, a
  fellowship, a grant, free credits, a waitlist.
- the post is the publisher's own DECISION, POSITION, EVALUATION or REPORT about its
  product or about the field: ads in its product, a pricing or access change, handing a
  model to selected partners, a safety or capability evaluation, a letter to a
  government, a policy stance, a data report on how people use the product, or an
  executive's lessons from building the thing. Nothing shipped, but the company itself
  moved, and that is an event.
- a customer story counts too when the customer is a name MOST PEOPLE WOULD RECOGNISE:
  a household brand, a big public company, a widely used product. Only a company nobody
  can place makes it an ad.
- AN INTERVIEW OR A RECORDED CONVERSATION with a named figure — a founder, a CEO, a
  researcher, a former employee — about how the field is moving, what they are
  building, or what they got wrong. This is first-hand material nobody has written up
  yet, so it is ALWAYS KEEP. It is not the "executive essay" below: that is a company
  publishing its own polished reflection, this is a person answering questions.

DROP:

- a customer story: "how <company> built / achieved / accelerated X with <product>",
  a deployment write-up, an adoption or efficiency percentage. That is a case study,
  which is an ad with a customer's name on it.
- a how-to, tutorial, tip or best-practice guide for the publisher's OWN product
  ("how to schedule a report with <product>", "turn a spreadsheet into ...").
- an award, ranking or analyst report ("named a Leader in ...", Magic Quadrant).
- a partnership, funding round, datacentre, or a hiring note.
  (NOT a policy or regulatory letter — that is the publisher's own POSITION and is
  a KEEP above. The two lines contradicted each other for weeks, which is a coin
  flip, not a rule.)
- an executive essay, vision or values piece, or a "what building X taught me"
  reflection. An INTERVIEW is not this — see the keep list.
- a round-up of what was announced in some past month, or a conference recap.
- one clip of a series about a launch that already has its own post: keep the first,
  drop the rest.

When a post both announces a thing and dresses it in a customer's name, the ANNOUNCED
THING wins and you KEEP it. When in doubt, KEEP: a missed ad costs a scroll, a wrong
drop costs a post idea.

Return STRICT JSON, nothing else:
{"drop": [i, ...]}

`i` is the post's own index from the input. Return {"drop": []} when everything stays.
